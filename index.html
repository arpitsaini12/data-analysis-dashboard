How we define pressureA delivery is under pressure when a chase has a high required run rate, it falls in the final five overs, four or fewer wickets remain, or the match is a knockout or close finish. Your pressure lens can isolate each signal.
Explore methodology →

One game. Two baselines.

Batting: compare a player's strike rate on pressure deliveries with their strike rate on other deliveries. The index starts at 50; a positive change lifts the score and a negative change lowers it.

Bowling: compare economy rate per six legal balls in pressure and non-pressure overs. Lower pressure economy and wickets taken improve the score. Wides and no-balls are excluded from legal-ball counts.

Index & confidence: the impact is normalized onto a 0–100 scale centered at 50, then shrunk toward 50 when pressure or baseline samples are small. Rankings require at least 30 non-pressure balls as well as the selected pressure-ball threshold.

The index centers at 50, then weights the pressure-vs-baseline change by pressure and baseline sample confidence. The pressure definition is a transparent analytical lens, not a claim that every delivery has equal stakes. Chase thresholds adapt to match format; overlapping pressure signals count once in the default lens.
